Lee H. Skinner

Lee H. Skinner

fractal art

Biographical notes
Born 1939. A beloved and versatile fractal artist. Has been called the godfather of fractal art for his contributions to the field. Possibly archives the world's largest collection of fractals. Works as programmer and mathematician in Albuquerque, NM. For many years programmed groundwater models for Sandia National Laboratories. Authored HP-35 Math Pac for Hewlitt-Packard, a manual of algorithms. Original developer of several formula types for Fractint, the comprehensive fractal-generating program. Interests include mathematical games, cosmology, the new physics, topographical maps, photogrammetry, wilderness hiking, caving and classical music. Falsely rumored to keep two or more computers going continuously. Has generated thousands of his own fractals, often at great depth, many hundreds of which have been published on CD-ROMS.

Commentary
His images reflect Skinner's mathematical skill and input often to several decimal places. Thus Skinner visits fractal terrain where other fractal artists never have the patience or courage to go. It may take Skinner many hours to render one of his fractals, even on a fast PC, because of the complexity of the mathematical calculations required to color each pixel. "1mntg010," small circles hurtling in straight lines and receding to an infinite horizon, is a dramatic composition in lineal perspective. It uses a strong palette of reds, oranges and blues. "1zem1008", more complex, is also linearly composed but flowingly, as if to represent mesh or fabric on a loom. Its modest palette of tans, oranges and browns seems chosen to reinforce its fluid design. "1wood04," is more conventional, effective for its visual depth simulation, and bright blues and reds.
