Sylvie Gallet, Part 1

Sylvie Gallet, Part 1

fractal art

COMMENTARY AND BIOGRAPHICAL NOTES
Fractals is a mathematical art, attracting both mathematicians and artists to its study and practice. Sylvie Gallet wears hats in both camps. As mathemetician and programmer, she is perhaps the world's foremost master of the writing of fractal formulas--sets of equations that define and determine fractal structure--and such prominant fractal artists as Bob Carr, Lee H. Skinner and others have regularly relied upon her formulas to draw their own fractals. But rare for a mathemetician, Sylvie Gallet is a fractal artist of the very first order. Based almost invariably on her own formulas, Gallet has produced a large portfolio of innovative and dramatic fractals. Exploring frequently beyond the fractal cliches of spiral and disk, she provides an entirely new world for us of fractal objects and forms that have never been viewed before.

Gallet's palette complements her images. Her colors are invariably muted, even bland, sometimes sand- or clay-colored. She often relies on small variations of the same color, instead of a grand application of intense color. One searches her fractals in vain for anything less than the perfect marriage color and form. Of all the fractal artists working today, she may be one of the few to have fashioned and refined a fractal style unique unto itself.

Gallet lives and works near Paris, where she has taught mathematics to 11-15 year-olds for 23 years. She holds an advanced math degree and is an experienced computer programmer in Basic, Pascal and Assembly Language. Besides math and fractals, she is a lover of classical music, German and Russian opera, cats and puzzles. She admires the work of such artists as Escher, Magritte, Vasarely, Cezanne, Van Gogh, Calder and Christie. She discovered fractals as early as 1985 but her first PC didn't arrive til 1992. She tells us that writing formulas is an important part of her creativity.
