Categories

Contest administrators understand that computer art does not
necessarily accommodate itself into categories. Nonetheless,
for contest purposes, administrators find it useful
to classify images in five different still categories and in
a separate animation category.

Computer-painted and -drawn art
These may be works largely created in such draw-and-paint programs
as Fractal Painter, CorelDraw, Photoshop and others,
sometimes requiring a stylus and drawing tablet, and often
requiring traditional artistic skills.

Fractal and mathematical art
Works generally drawn and colored by input into algorithmic
and/or formula-driven programs, such as UltraFractal, Fractint,
XenoDream, Tiera-Zon and a host of other fractal-generating engines,
some of which may be self-programmed by the artist.

Computer-enhanced photography
Photos taken by digital or conventional cameras that are manipulated
and enhanced in Photoshop or other image-editing and drawing programs.

3-D rendered art
Art that simulates three dimensions. These may be produced in
such programs as 3-D Max, Bryce, Poser, POV-Ray and others. These
programs may use the effects of shadow and light to create a
kind of photo-realism, and to create strange and unusual landscapes
and environments. May be populated with human or animal figures.

A general and all-inclusive category
This category includes collages, scans, mixed-media and other works
not easily categorized. If you do not know where your image fits,
it probably fits here.

Animation
Works produced in Flash (SWF) or GIF format.
These may be of large file size. If an artist wishes to submit
in a different animation format, please contact
the contest administrators.

Categories are important because entries are exhibited and judged
by category. Contestants are therefore required to submit images
by category, although contest administrators will feel free to move
an image into a different category if it seems appropriate.
