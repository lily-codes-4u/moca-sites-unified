SOUP DU JOUR

Andy Warhol. American. 1928-1987. Campbell Soup can. Silk screen on canvas.

This particular image, among the others that follow in this gallery, while taken with Don's digital camera, is not of Andy Warhol's famous picture, but is rather of the Campbell Soup can itself, taken in Don's studio (then of course worked and re-worked in the computer). Andy told us that we would all have our 15 minutes of fame but, alas, I am still waiting for my 15 minutes. I prefer his profounder, "Business is the best art."

Andy had a genius for promotion and a fetish for celebrity. He operated the Factory, which turned out his work, produced a magazine on newsprint called Interview, developed a coterie of worshipful hangers-on, was shot and badly injured in mid-career by one of his crazed female associates, and took a positively un-trendy can of popular soup and turned it into improbable art. He showed umpteen versions of it simultaneously, all of which were identical. He did the same with other cultural icons, including Marilyn Monroe. It pretended to be great art, through no fault of his. The Philistines have never forgiven him. He was a loner, a legend and a cult figure in his last years, and his work sold for very big money. He died ingloriously in a New York hospital after a botched gall bladder operation. The Andy Warhol museum in Pittsburgh, his hometown, celebrates his art.
